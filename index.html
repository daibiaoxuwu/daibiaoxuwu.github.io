I am a Research Associate in the Department of Computer Science and Engineering at Michigan State University, advised by Dr. Zhichao Cao.

I earned my Ph.D. in Software Engineering from Tsinghua University in 2025, where I was advised by Professor Yunhao Liu. I also received my Bachelor of Engineering in Computer Science and Technology from Tsinghua University in 2020.

My research focuses on the intersection of the Internet of Things and Artificial Intelligence. I build physics-informed intelligent signal processing for low-power wide-area networks: receivers that model what is known about the signal and use compact learning only for what cannot be modeled, so that weak and distorted LoRa signals can be recovered on commodity devices.

DeRa: Deep-Range Low-Power Wide Area IoT via Ultra-sensitive Signal Detection

Jialuo Du, Jingkai Lin, Zhichao Cao

MobiCom 2026ACM International Conference on Mobile Computing and Networking

Weak LoRa packets are lost at detection, before decoding even starts. Carrier frequency offset rotates the phase of every preamble chirp, so existing detectors add only magnitudes and waste signal power. DeRa models that phase evolution, compensates the CFO-induced timing, frequency and leakage distortions (up to 40 kHz CFO), and accumulates the preamble coherently. On COTS devices across SF 7–12 it lowers the detection threshold by 4.3–4.8 dB and the decoding threshold by 3.3–3.9 dB.

LoRaTrimmer: Optimal Energy Condensation with Chirp Trimming for LoRa Weak Signal Decoding

Jialuo Du, Yunhao Liu, Yidong Ren, Li Liu, Zhichao Cao

MobiCom 2024ACM International Conference on Mobile Computing and Networking

Real LoRa symbols contain a frequency jump that standard dechirp-and-FFT demodulation ignores, adding noise and a random phase jump. LoRaTrimmer trims the FFT window at the jump and handles the phase jump probabilistically, gaining 1.70–2.49 dB SNR over the state of the art (up to 1.67× coverage area). Splitting the DFT matrix into two anti-diagonal triangles computes every bin's trimmed FFT at once, in O(n3/2) time.
